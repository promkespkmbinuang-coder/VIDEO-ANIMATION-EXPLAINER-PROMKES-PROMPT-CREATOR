# Health Integrity & Source Control

## Claim decomposition

Break material statements into atomic propositions. Preserve exact wording and source location when possible. Mark each as health/medical, institutional/service, date/time/location, behavioral instruction, statistic, cause/effect, promise, or creative narrative.

## Evidence quality dimensions

Check relevance, directness, method quality, coverage, independence, consistency, currency, transparency, jurisdiction and intended population. An official source is not automatically sufficient for every claim; assess whether it answers the proposition in this setting. Multiple copies of one original report are not independent corroboration.

## Safe evidence hierarchy

Prefer current official Indonesian national/regional health guidance for local operational information; WHO and authoritative technical guidance where globally relevant; peer-reviewed evidence or recognized clinical/public-health guidance for technical claims. Note when the material's jurisdiction, date or population differs. Do not overstate what a source supports.

## Claim statuses

- `VERIFIED`: directly inspected suitable evidence supports the proposition in the relevant scope.
- `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`: evidence supports only part or a narrower scope.
- `MIXED_OR_CONTESTED`: credible evidence/source conflict exists.
- `BELUM_DIVERIFIKASI`: not independently checked or access unavailable.
- `CONTRADICTED`: relevant stronger evidence conflicts with claim.
- `UNKNOWN`: proposition cannot be interpreted because source material is missing/illegible.

## Truth-before-creativity rules

1. Do not invent diagnoses, symptoms, prevalence, statistics, doses, benefits, risks, transmission routes, causal mechanisms or treatment instructions.
2. Do not upgrade uncertainty, correlation or association into causation.
3. Do not promise cure, prevention, safety or outcome absolutely.
4. Do not dramatize a claim beyond the evidence scope.
5. Keep the original health fact as-is or simplify with equivalent meaning; record changed wording.
6. Label metaphors and fictional scenarios as creative, and ensure they do not imply additional medical facts.
7. If a health claim is material but not verified, say so and do not make it stronger in the VO/overlay.
8. For individual health requests, do not diagnose or prescribe; point to appropriate qualified care.

## Source record

`SRC-ID | author/issuer | title | URL/locator | source type | published/updated date | data period | jurisdiction | method/scope | access state | limitations`.

## Claim-evidence record

`CLM-ID | precise proposition | source ref | evidence ref | support/contra | scope/time | evidence state | safe wording | affected scenes`.

## Privacy and consent

Do not expose private patient/client identifiers in examples, screenshots, tests or GitHub commits. Minimize personal data, avoid unnecessary sensitive details and use a fictional representation unless use of a real person's image/voice is appropriately authorized. A public poster image can still contain personal data or rights-restricted assets.
